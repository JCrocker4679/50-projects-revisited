# Blog Vision & Audience

## Who is this for?

### Audience 1: Junior devs who've done the tutorials
People who can follow a coding tutorial and get something working, but don't know how to make the leap from "I built a thing" to "I work on a team that ships products." The message is that simple code implementation isn't really enough any more — you need to be thinking about the whole end-to-end chain of a business.

**The analogy:** Being able to cook an incredible omelette or burger isn't really enough for a restaurant any more. You need to be thinking about where the food is sourced, how it's cooked sustainably, all the ingredients that go into it — that's what sets you apart. It's premium quality output vs just some commoditised thing anyone can do.

The blog shows them what that looks like in practice: taking a tutorial project and thinking about it the way a PM and a development team would. Error handling, accessibility, user stories, analytics, marketing, the lot.

### Audience 2: Busy dads / exhausted side hustlers
People with the same time constraints as me — figuring out what they can realistically do in their spare time using agentic AI workflows. Maybe their side business benefits, maybe their full-time job benefits, doesn't really matter. The appeal is to people who don't have 8 hours a day to sit and code, but want to see what's possible when you combine domain knowledge with AI tooling.

## What this is NOT
- Not a coding tutorial (I don't have that expertise and that's not the point)
- Not "look how clever AI is"
- It IS: "here's what happens when you bring product thinking + AI to old tutorial code"

## What we need to capture along the way

### For each project session:
- The prompts used (what we asked the AI, how we steered it)
- The setup / workflow (how Claude Code is configured, what an agentic workflow looks like)
- What worked, what didn't, what surprised us
- The before/after of the code (but the focus is on the *thinking*, not the syntax)

### For the blog/video series:
- First piece needs to be an introduction: what we're doing and why, the setup, explaining the concept
- Description for the blog explaining the premise to new readers
- Each project becomes a case study, not a tutorial
- Show things happening live — the prompts, the iteration, the decisions

## Content creation approach

### The Traversy method (adapted)
Do the project first — messy, exploratory, figuring things out. Then reflect on what happened and what mattered. Then go back and re-do the prompts and planning stages live for the recording, working backwards from the finished product. This means:

1. **Do the work** — iterate with AI, make mistakes, get to a finished state
2. **Reflect** — what decisions mattered? What was surprising? What would you tell someone?
3. **Re-record** — walk through it again cleanly, knowing where it ends up

The AI will probably give different output the second time round, and that's fine — actually interesting in itself. The point is the thinking and the process, not reproducing exact outputs.

### Raw vs edited
The re-recorded version IS the edited version. The raw exploratory work stays in the notes for reference but doesn't need to be published. If something genuinely surprising or instructive happens in the raw session, pull that out as a highlight.

## Platform

### Primary: YouTube
Video-first makes sense for this kind of content — screen recordings of AI interaction, walking through code, showing the before/after. YouTube is where the developer content audience already lives.

### Supporting channels (TBD)
The landscape for written/social promotion has shifted — Twitter/X isn't the obvious "just post there" answer it used to be. Worth exploring but not overthinking right now. Options to revisit later: Dev.to, Substack, LinkedIn, Bluesky, Threads. A simple blog on a personal site as the canonical home isn't a bad shout either.

### Don't overthink distribution yet
The content needs to exist before distribution matters. Start making things, figure out where to put them later.

## Cadence
Hobby project. No pressure, no schedule. Ship when something's ready.
